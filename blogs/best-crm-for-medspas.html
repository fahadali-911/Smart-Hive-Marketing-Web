CRM & Patient Journey

Best CRM for Med Spas: GoHighLevel vs Boulevard vs PatientNow, Aesthetic Record & More

Choosing a MedSpa CRM is not simply a software-shopping decision. The real question is which parts of your patient journey your technology actually needs to manage.

SmartHive Marketing13 min read

A MedSpa can be generating leads every week and still have a growth problem.

The ads may be working. People may be visiting treatment pages. Forms may be getting submitted. The front desk may even feel busy. But somewhere between “I’m interested” and “I’m coming back for my next treatment,” patients can disappear.

That is why choosing the best CRM for a MedSpa is not simply a software-shopping decision. The real question is: which parts of your patient journey does your technology actually need to manage?

That matters because platforms such as GoHighLevel, Boulevard, PatientNow, Aesthetic Record, Zenoti and Vagaro do not all solve the same problem.

HighLevel is heavily oriented toward lead capture, pipelines, communication, marketing and automation. Boulevard emphasizes scheduling, client experience and self-care business operations. PatientNow and Aesthetic Record go deeper into aesthetic-practice and clinical workflows. Zenoti combines clinical and operational capabilities with a strong multi-location orientation, while Vagaro blends booking, business management, marketing and increasingly substantial MedSpa-specific clinical features.

So there is no honest universal answer to “Which CRM is best?” There is, however, a much better way to choose.

What Does a MedSpa Actually Need From a CRM?

Before comparing platforms, map the jobs the technology needs to perform. A typical MedSpa patient journey might look like this:

  1. 01Discover
  2. 02Inquire
  3. 03Respond
  4. 04Book
  5. 05Consult
  6. 06Treat
  7. 07Follow Up
  8. 08Rebook
  9. 09Retain
  10. 10Reactivate

Different software platforms own different parts of that path.

Acquisition and lead capture

You need to know where inquiries came from and make sure forms, calls, ads and landing pages do not create disconnected lead records.

Response

A lead sitting untouched in an inbox is not much different from a lead you never generated. Your system may need two-way messaging, automated responses, routing, reminders or a visible sales pipeline.

Booking

Once someone is interested, how easily can that person move into a confirmed consultation or treatment? This is where scheduling platforms often become more important than a traditional sales CRM.

Consultation and treatment

Medical aesthetics adds another layer. Charts, consent forms, treatment plans, clinical notes, before-and-after photography and other patient records may need systems designed for healthcare or aesthetics—not simply marketing software.

Follow-up and retention

After treatment, the system may need to support reminders, membership activity, rebooking, review requests and appropriate reactivation campaigns.

Measurement

Finally, management needs to understand where people are falling out of the journey. A CRM that captures every lead but tells you nothing about treatment retention may still leave a major blind spot.

CRM vs Practice Management vs Marketing Automation vs EMR

A lot of confusion in the MedSpa software market comes from using “CRM” as a label for almost everything. They overlap, but they are not the same.

01CRM
A customer relationship management system generally helps manage contacts, leads, communication, pipelines and relationship history. For a MedSpa, this can be especially useful before an inquiry becomes a booked patient.
02Practice-management software
Practice-management systems typically go deeper into day-to-day operations: schedules, providers, services, payments, memberships, staff, client profiles and other operational workflows.
03Marketing automation
Marketing automation focuses on what happens around acquisition and follow-up: forms, landing pages, email, SMS, segmentation, nurture sequences, attribution and reactivation.
04EMR/EHR
Clinical record systems handle a different responsibility. They can include medical charting, treatment documentation, photos, consent forms, prescriptions and other clinical information.

Some modern MedSpa platforms combine several of these categories. That is why feature-count comparisons can be misleading. A platform with fewer marketing features may still be far more appropriate if clinical documentation is the real requirement.

01 — HighLevel

GoHighLevel for MedSpas

What it is

HighLevel is best understood as a sales, marketing and automation platform rather than a purpose-built MedSpa clinical system.

Its current platform includes CRM and pipelines, forms, surveys, funnels, website tools, unified conversations, email and SMS marketing, workflow automation, booking calendars, reputation management, reporting and other growth-oriented functionality.

Where it fits in a MedSpa

HighLevel can make sense when the biggest problems occur before the appointment. For example: a Meta lead comes in. The contact enters a pipeline. An immediate text goes out. The team receives an alert. The patient is reminded to schedule. Unbooked leads enter follow-up, and older leads can later be segmented for reactivation.

That is the type of workflow HighLevel is designed to handle well. Its current feature set also includes missed-call text-back, appointment reminders, sales pipelines, workflows, reputation tools and database reactivation functionality.

Where it may fit best

HighLevel deserves serious consideration when your primary constraint is:

  • slow lead response,
  • fragmented marketing follow-up,
  • weak pipeline visibility,
  • landing-page conversion,
  • lead nurturing,
  • review generation,
  • or reactivation.

But a marketing CRM should not automatically be treated as the system that replaces your full clinical record.

HighLevel also states that its accounts are not HIPAA compliant by default; it offers a separate HIPAA package that includes a BAA and additional safeguards. Practices handling PHI should evaluate the exact configuration and responsibilities carefully rather than assuming the platform name alone solves compliance.

02 — Boulevard

Boulevard for MedSpas

Boulevard approaches the problem from a different direction. Its platform is heavily centered around booking, client experience and self-care business operations.

Boulevard currently lists online booking, scheduling controls, reminders, waitlists, resource scheduling, deposits, client profiles, conversation history, two-way texting and memberships among its capabilities. Its broader positioning also includes payments, marketing and loyalty functionality.

That makes Boulevard particularly interesting for a MedSpa where the front-of-house experience matters deeply. Think: a patient discovers the practice, chooses a treatment and books through a polished scheduling flow, while their client information, communication and membership relationship stays connected to that experience. This is fundamentally different from starting with a sales pipeline.

Where it may fit best

Boulevard may make sense when your priority is:

  • premium booking experience,
  • scheduling,
  • client communication,
  • memberships,
  • front-desk operations,
  • retention,
  • and a polished self-care customer journey.

If your biggest issue is sophisticated lead pipelines from advertising campaigns, you should compare Boulevard’s marketing capabilities against a marketing-first CRM rather than assuming the two products are direct replacements.

03 — PatientNow

PatientNow

PatientNow moves much further toward the clinical and practice-management side of aesthetics.

Its current platform connects EMR, scheduling, payments, marketing, memberships, photo documentation and other practice-management functions. It specifically positions itself around aesthetic medicine and MedSpas rather than general sales teams or generic service businesses.

PatientNow lists aesthetic charting, digital intake, patient records, before-and-after photo documentation, online booking, reminders, rebooking, marketing campaigns, payments, memberships and reporting among its current capabilities.

Where it may fit best

PatientNow deserves evaluation when you want more of the MedSpa operation living inside one environment. For example, patient record + charting + photos + booking + memberships + payment + marketing, rather than using one platform mainly for lead nurturing and another for almost everything after booking.

Practices with more involved clinical documentation requirements should pay particular attention to this distinction.

04 — Aesthetic Record

Aesthetic Record

Aesthetic Record is another platform built specifically around the aesthetics industry.

Its current positioning describes an all-in-one system incorporating EMR, scheduling, inventory, CRM, payments, messaging and marketing capabilities, with workflows designed for aesthetic and wellness practices.

This makes Aesthetic Record fundamentally different from a general-purpose marketing CRM. The important question here is not “Does it have a CRM?” The more useful question is: how much of my aesthetic practice do I want operating inside the same clinical and business system?

Where it may fit best

Aesthetic Record may be particularly relevant for practices that value:

  • aesthetics-specific workflows,
  • clinical documentation,
  • scheduling,
  • patient management,
  • treatment records,
  • and practice operations.

If your practice already runs effectively on an aesthetic EMR but struggles with top-of-funnel follow-up, the answer might not be replacing it. The answer may be connecting it to a stronger marketing layer. That distinction can save a lot of unnecessary migration pain.

05 — Zenoti

Zenoti

Zenoti sits toward the broader, more operational end of the spectrum.

Its MedSpa platform currently combines clinical charting, documentation, before-and-after photos, booking, payments, memberships and marketing. Zenoti also emphasizes centralized operational management and multi-location capabilities.

That makes it particularly relevant when a practice has moved beyond the needs of one simple calendar and a basic lead list.

Where it may fit best

Zenoti is worth evaluating for:

  • growing MedSpa groups,
  • multi-location operations,
  • centralized scheduling,
  • membership management,
  • payments,
  • clinical workflows,
  • marketing,
  • inventory and staff operations,
  • management reporting.

A single-provider boutique clinic and a rapidly expanding multi-site group have very different technology requirements. Zenoti’s broader operating model is therefore more important to understand than simply asking whether it “has CRM.”

06 — Vagaro

Vagaro

Vagaro has historically been widely associated with beauty, wellness and appointment-based businesses, but its current MedSpa offering goes considerably further.

Its medical-spa platform currently promotes online booking, EMR functionality, custom charting, SOAP notes, patient records, treatment plans, consent forms, before-and-after photos, payments, memberships, packages, marketing, reporting and multi-location management.

Vagaro also offers email/text marketing capabilities, loyalty programs, inventory, online booking and business-management functionality.

Where it may fit best

Vagaro may appeal to practices looking for a broad combination of booking + client management + clinical tools + payments + memberships + marketing without building a large collection of separate systems.

As with every platform in this article, though, a demo should focus on your actual workflows rather than the vendor’s feature checklist.

Which MedSpa CRM Is Best for Your Practice?

The useful answer depends on where your patient journey is breaking.

If lead generation and marketing automation are the problem

A marketing-oriented platform such as HighLevel deserves evaluation—especially if you need stronger pipelines, SMS/email nurture, forms, funnel pages, follow-up workflows and reactivation.

If booking and client experience are the priority

Boulevard is worth examining closely. Its scheduling and client-experience orientation is materially different from a traditional marketing CRM.

If aesthetic-specific clinical workflows matter most

Look carefully at PatientNow and Aesthetic Record. Both are built much closer to the clinical reality of aesthetic medicine than a general-purpose sales CRM.

If you operate multiple locations

Zenoti should be on the shortlist, particularly where centralized operations, memberships, staff, reporting, booking and clinical workflows need to scale together. PatientNow and Vagaro also advertise capabilities relevant to expanding and multi-location practices.

If you already have good practice software but leads are still slipping away

Do not automatically replace the whole system. You may need a second layer.

Your CRM Does Not Have to Do Everything

This is where many MedSpa software decisions go wrong. Owners often search for one platform that will do absolutely everything: advertising, landing pages, CRM, scheduling, EMR, payments, memberships, marketing and reporting.

Sometimes that is possible. Sometimes forcing everything into one platform creates a worse system. A connected technology stack might instead look like this:

  1. Google / Meta / Search
  2. Treatment Landing Page
  3. Marketing CRM
  4. Lead Follow-Up
  5. Booking Platform
  6. Practice Management / EMR
  7. Treatment
  8. Retention & Reactivation

The critical issue is not how many logos appear in your software stack. It is whether information moves correctly between them.

10 Questions to Ask Before Choosing a MedSpa CRM

Before booking demos, answer these internally:

  1. 01

    Where do our inquiries currently come from?

  2. 02

    How long does it usually take us to respond to a new inquiry?

  3. 03

    Do we need to manage leads before they become patients?

  4. 04

    Which system should own appointment scheduling?

  5. 05

    What happens automatically after someone submits a form?

  6. 06

    Where should clinical information and PHI live?

  7. 07

    How do we identify leads that never booked?

  8. 08

    How do we handle rebooking, memberships and appropriate reactivation?

  9. 09

    Which existing systems must integrate with the new platform?

  10. 10

    What numbers does management actually need to see every week?

If you cannot answer those questions, comparing software demos becomes difficult because every vendor presentation will look impressive. Define the job first. Then evaluate the software.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best CRM for a MedSpa?

There is no single best platform for every MedSpa. HighLevel may be attractive when lead management and marketing automation are the main problems, while platforms such as PatientNow or Aesthetic Record may be more appropriate when clinical and aesthetics-specific workflows are central. Boulevard, Zenoti and Vagaro solve different combinations of booking, operations, client management and retention.

Is GoHighLevel good for MedSpas?

It can be a strong marketing and lead-management platform for MedSpas because it supports CRM pipelines, forms, funnels, communication, workflow automation, calendars and reputation tools. It should not automatically be treated as a replacement for a dedicated MedSpa EMR or clinical practice-management system.

Is Boulevard a CRM?

Boulevard includes CRM-like client-management capabilities, including client profiles, conversation history, messaging and memberships, but its broader positioning is centered around scheduling, client experience, payments and self-care business operations rather than a traditional sales CRM.

What is the difference between MedSpa CRM and practice-management software?

CRM software generally focuses more heavily on contacts, leads, communication and pipelines. Practice-management software usually handles more of the day-to-day business operation, such as appointments, services, staff, payments and patient/client records. MedSpa-specific platforms increasingly combine both.

Can a MedSpa use more than one software platform?

Yes. In many cases, using a marketing CRM alongside a practice-management or EMR platform can be more practical than replacing a strong clinical system solely because the marketing workflow is weak.

Do MedSpas need an EMR and a CRM?

It depends on the practice and the information being handled. Clinical documentation and protected health information create different requirements from marketing lead management. A qualified compliance/legal review should determine how PHI is handled and which vendors need appropriate contractual safeguards.

Can CRM software automate MedSpa follow-up?

Many platforms now support some form of automated reminders, messaging, campaigns or re-engagement. The depth varies significantly, so the important question is whether the automation matches your real patient workflow rather than simply whether the vendor has an “automation” feature.

Before You Replace Your CRM, Find the Constraint

Switching CRM or practice-management systems can affect your team, your data and your daily workflow. So before replacing technology, identify what is actually broken.

Is it lead volume? Response time? Booking friction? Clinical workflow? Follow-up? Retention? Reporting? Or simply poor integration between tools you already have?
